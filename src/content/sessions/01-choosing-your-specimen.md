## Before the session

Find three restaurants that have actually closed — permanently, verifiably,
somewhere you can check. A "temporarily closed" listing or a relocation
doesn't qualify; you need a death, not a pause. For each, note what's
publicly findable in ten minutes: a last review, a news mention, a health
department record, a delivery-app page that still loads.

## In the session

You pitch your three candidates in ninety seconds each; the room (not just
the tutor) votes on which one has the deepest trail to work with, because a
specimen with three sources and one with thirty produce very different
semesters. Renata explains the one disqualifying case: closures that were
really corporate decisions — a chain divesting a market — rather than a
single location failing on its own terms. Those get vetoed on the spot,
however interesting.

## Afterwards

You leave with one confirmed specimen, logged with the tutor so no one else
in the room is dissecting the same restaurant, and a reading list of what
kind of evidence to start collecting before next week's session, when the
case file actually gets built.
