Desmond spent seven years as a municipal health inspector, which mostly
means he has read more restaurant kitchens' worst days than their owners
would like. He teaches students to treat a health-inspection record, a
one-star review and a delivery app's cached menu as witness statements —
each partial, each with its own reason to exaggerate or stay quiet — rather
than as gossip to be believed or dismissed on the spot.

He runs the weeks built on evidence you have to interpret rather than sum:
building the case file, tracing the review history for the point sentiment
actually turned, reconstructing a service failure from conflicting
accounts, and the bias-hunting pass on why the Autopsy Report often lies to
its own author before anyone else reads it.
