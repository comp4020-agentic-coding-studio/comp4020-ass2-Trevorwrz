## The brief

> The case for why your specimen closed, built so a stranger could follow it
> without having read anything else you've produced this semester.

This is the load-bearing piece of the semester — the dossier proved you had
a real specimen, this proves you can build a defensible explanation from it.
"Defensible" is doing the work in that sentence: a report that only ever
argues for itself, and never puts a competing explanation in the room to
rule it out, has not done the job this course is teaching.

## What "competing explanations" means here

Every restaurant closure has at least three plausible stories on offer
before you dig — bad location, bad food, bad luck. Naming the ones that
don't hold up, and showing why, is worth more marks than a longer list of
evidence for the one you believe.

## What you submit

A written report, any reasonable length, structured so the verdict comes
first and the evidence follows it — not the reverse. Attach or summarise the
week 9 cross-examination: what a peer challenged, and what you did about
it.
