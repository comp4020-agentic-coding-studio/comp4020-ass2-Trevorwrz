# Policies and support

## Choosing a specimen

Your restaurant must have a verifiable closure date, an independently
findable address, and at least three sources that don't all trace back to
the same person (a review site, a news mention, a health-inspection record,
a delivery-app cache, a "permanently closed" map listing). A restaurant that
closed because its parent chain collapsed for unrelated reasons is not
eligible — the point is a single location's failure, not a corporate
decision made somewhere else. Once you've cleared your specimen with a
tutor in week 1, you can't swap it later in the semester; a mid-semester
swap resets your Specimen Dossier mark to zero, because half a semester of
compounding evidence is the thing being marked.

## Sourcing and citation

Every claim in your Autopsy Report needs a source a marker can check —
a link, a screenshot with its URL and access date, or an archive.org
capture. An uncited claim about why the restaurant closed is treated as
speculation, not evidence, however plausible it sounds.

## Extensions

Ask before a deadline, not after it. An extension arranged in advance is
routine; a submission that simply arrives late is not accepted. Say what
happened and how much time you need — a specific request is easy to grant
quickly.

## Academic integrity

The specimen must be a real, independently verifiable restaurant. Inventing
a restaurant, or inventing evidence about a real one, is not a shortcut
through the workload — it's the one thing this course cannot be about and
still be this course. Declare any AI assistance the same way the rest of
COMP4020 does: what you asked for, and what you kept.

## Getting help

Stuck on a source that won't resolve, a lens that isn't landing on your
specimen, or a disagreement with a peer reviewer you can't settle
yourselves? Ask in the week's Autopsy first — that's what the time is for —
or email a tutor listed on the [people page](/people/).
